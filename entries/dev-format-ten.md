# NOMAD — Ten Critiques on FORMAT (of v1.3)

Hard read of the screenplay's formatting and presentation. Each lens names the actual offending lines. Fixes applied in v1.4.

---

**1. WGA / Final Draft purist.**
The rendered script is supposed to be a clean screenplay, but it's riddled with **authorial asides that belong in the writer's notes, not on the page**: "(Cairns is already an ad and doesn't know it — he prices everything.)", "(The machine already doubts the real one.)", "the one active choice he makes in the film, and it's this," "We're in her frame, not Will's," "The same pitch Cairns once made to Will. John is the next Will." A screenplay SHOWS; it never explains itself to the reader. Strip every one.

**2. Festival submission screener (judges professionalism at a glance).**
**Bold mini-headers inside action** — "**The apartment.**", "**Hers, not his.**" — are not screenplay format; they read as an outline that forgot to become a script. Either promote them to real sluglines or delete the bold. Right now a reader clocks "amateur" in three seconds.

**3. 1st AD (breaks it down to schedule).**
The **INTERCUT is malformed.** "THE DOUBLE RITUAL — INTERCUT" is a section title, not a scene heading, and then it alternates full sluglines with no time tags on the repeats. I can't board this. Use the convention: establish both locations, then "INTERCUT:" and let the alternation run — with CONTINUOUS where needed.

**4. Script supervisor (continuity of headings).**
Time-of-day is **inconsistent**: some sluglines carry DAY/NIGHT/CONTINUOUS, the prologue and the intercut carry none. The PROLOGUE has **no scene heading at all** — it opens on an image with no slug. Every scene needs INT/EXT · LOCATION · TIME. No exceptions.

**5. Reader / coverage (white space, density).**
Several **action paragraphs run 5–7 lines** — the render farm, the office morning, the Camila viewing. Pro action is 1–4 lines then a break. Dense blocks read as slow and unshot. Break them into beats; the white space IS pacing.

**6. Subtitle stickler (bilingual convention).**
"*(subtitled)*" is informal and inconsistent. Standard is a parenthetical **"(in Spanish; subtitled)"** under the cue, every time, so the reader and the mix know exactly what's spoken and what's read. Apply to the girl and to Camila identically.

**7. Dialogue-format checker.**
The parenthetical is **buried inside the line**: "...checked I was — (waving it off) I don't even remember my own login." A parenthetical gets its **own line** under the cue, not mid-sentence. Also "GIRL" as a speaking cue for a named-enough part should be specified (GIRL / GIRL 1) for the AD and the cast list.

**8. The hybrid-doc problem (the deepest note).**
The film keeps two things in one file: a *screenplay* and a *director's intent doc*. On the page they bleed — motivation ("becoming the thing that pushed him out of his own city") sits in action lines as if it were shootable. Decide the artifact: the RENDERED SCRIPT should be pure screenplay; ALL intent lives in the master draft. This is the same split the site already claims but the script doesn't yet honor.

**9. Transition / title conventions.**
"TITLE: NOMAD" is loose — use "MAIN TITLE" or "SMASH TO TITLE." INSERTs are close but inconsistent ("INSERT — a scanner's green line..." vs "INSERT — STATUS:..."). Pick one INSERT grammar and, if you're strict, close them with BACK TO SCENE. "CUT TO BLACK." is correct.

**10. Contrarian craft veteran.**
The format is trying to have it both ways — arty prose blocks AND screenplay cues — and lands in an uncanny valley that flatters neither. Commit. If it's a shooting-adjacent script, cut the poetry and trust sluglines; if it's a lyrical treatment, drop the character cues. My call: **make the rendered script a real screenplay** (cues, clean action, no asides) and let the master hold the lyricism. The site's whole premise (draft = source, script = build) demands it.

---

## Consensus → applied in v1.4 (the rendered script only; master keeps the intent)

1. **Stripped every authorial aside** from the script — no more explaining the film to the reader.
2. **Killed the bold mini-headers**; folded into action or real sluglines.
3. **Rebuilt the intercut** properly: two locations established, then INTERCUT, alternation with CONTINUOUS.
4. **Gave the prologue a slug** (EXT. INFINITY POOL — GOLDEN HOUR) and audited every heading for INT/EXT · LOCATION · TIME.
5. **Broke long action into short beats** (render farm, office, Camila) for white space.
6. **Standardized subtitles** to "(in Spanish; subtitled)".
7. **Moved the inline parenthetical** onto its own line; specified GIRL 1.
8. **INSERT grammar unified**; "MAIN TITLE: NOMAD."
9. Net effect: the script now reads as a professional screenplay; all directorial intent stays in the master draft where it belongs.
