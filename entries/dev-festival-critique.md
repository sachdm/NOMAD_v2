# NOMAD — Ten Adjudicator / Festival Critiques (of v1.1)

Ten distinct programmers and jurors read the short. Each: verdict, the single most useful note. Consensus synthesized at the bottom; changes applied in v1.2.

---

**1. Sundance programmer (US indie — character access, originality).**
Verdict: strong concept, and the friendship gives it a heart most tech shorts lack. Concern: Will is almost entirely *passive* — things happen to him, he drifts. Sundance wants a protagonist who makes at least one active, revealing choice we can hang our empathy on. Right now his only real action is going to "report it" at the end. Note: **give Will one earlier active choice that costs him something** — ideally a small betrayal of John or of a local — so the ending lands on a person, not a leaf.

**2. Cannes / Semaine de la Critique selector (auteur, form, politics).**
Verdict: formally intelligent (the 0.4 motif, the render-farm reveal). But the film flirts with committing the exact sin it critiques — it colonizes Mexico City as backdrop. The locals are subtitled *texture*, never subjects. Note: **the Mexican side needs one real human with interiority and agency**, or the film is tourism about tourism. Give the city a face that looks back.

**3. Acquisitions exec (A24 / Neon prestige-genre).**
Verdict: castable, ownable, feature-legible. The comp everyone will reach for is Black Mirror, and that's a ceiling. What's NOT Black Mirror here is the *grief* — a man loses his best friend twice. Lean into that; it's the differentiator. Note: **the last image should be the friendship, not the tech.** Currently the stutter frame competes with John's face. Choose John.

**4. Clermont-Ferrand juror (world's biggest short fest — economy, justification of short form).**
Verdict: it earns short form, but it's saggy in the middle. Two office-desk scenes and three apartment-night scenes create repetition; the audience gets the erosion fast. Note: **cut ~90 seconds of the middle** — compress the office/apartment beats so momentum doesn't stall before Mexico.

**5. Academy Nicholl reader (screenplay craft, clarity on the page).**
Verdict: clean, professional, but the midpoint tell — the linchpin — is now written as "correct but hollow, eyes going nowhere." On the page that's a direction, not a scene; a reader can't *feel* it. Note: **the tell needs a concrete, specific, legible beat** (an object, a fact, a physical action) so it plays on paper AND screen — not a blank the actor has to invent.

**6. Fantastic Fest / Sitges programmer (genre delivery, dread).**
Verdict: the render-farm and the verification-inversion are genuinely chilling — the best genre material. But the film under-builds the *rules* early, so the Act-8 inversion (machine flags the live human as fake) risks feeling like a twist rather than an inevitability. Note: **plant the tell and the "the model is the reference now" logic harder in Act 1** so the ending detonates instead of surprises.

**7. Morelia / Guadalajara programmer (Mexican festival — authenticity).**
Verdict: if this screens in Mexico as-is, the gentrification critique will read as an outsider scolding with Mexico as set-dressing. It needs Mexican authorship in its DNA. Note: **a Mexican co-writer is non-negotiable, and at least one scene must belong wholly to a local** — their language, their frame, their stakes — not filtered through Will.

**8. Social-realist / documentary juror (plausibility, earned politics).**
Verdict: the politics are real but abstract; we never feel a specific person displaced, only "gentrification" as concept. Note: **make the family Will displaces concrete and specific** — a face, a name, a single detail — so the theme has a body, not a thesis.

**9. Producer's rep / financier (budget, castability, feature path).**
Verdict: shootable on a tight budget (one office, apartments, one CDMX unit, a server room). Cairns is the award-bait role (two calibrated performances). Concern: the ending's ambiguity ("John almost catches it") may frustrate a market that wants a clean gut-punch. Note: **make sure the final beat is emotionally unambiguous even if narratively open** — the audience must feel the loss cleanly, whatever John "knows."

**10. Contrarian critic / jaded festival veteran.**
Verdict: "digital nomad gets replaced by AI" is a logline a hundred people are writing this year. What saves this one is specificity and the friendship — so protect those and kill anything generic. Note: **the storefront/Service and Michelle-as-CSR are your freshest satire; the poolside-margaritas nomad stuff is the most generic.** Trim the generic paradise, expand the specific mundane horror.

---

## Consensus (the notes that recurred)

- **The Mexican side is wallpaper** (2, 7, 8): needs one real local with interiority/agency; make the displaced family specific; commit to Mexican co-authorship.
- **Will is passive** (1, 9): give him one active, costly choice before the end.
- **The tell is a direction, not a scene** (5, 6): make it concrete and legible; plant its logic harder in Act 1 so the finale is inevitable, not a twist.
- **Protect the differentiator — grief, not tech** (3, 10): last image = John; trim the generic paradise; expand the specific satire.
- **The middle sags** (4, 10): compress office/apartment repetition.

## Changes applied in v1.2

1. Added a named local — CAMILA, the neighbor whose family Will's building displaced — recurring, with one scene that is wholly hers (subtitled, her frame). She's the city looking back.
2. Gave Will an **active, costly choice**: he knowingly takes the apartment Camila's family is being pushed out of — sees it, understands it, signs anyway. His passivity becomes complicity. (Addresses 1, 2, 7, 8 at once.)
3. Made the **tell concrete and legible**: Will asks Cairns to do the one specific physical thing only the real Cairns does (a private handshake / a stupid shared ritual) — the likeness performs it a half-beat wrong. Plays on the page and the screen; no blank.
4. **Planted the rule in Act 1**: at his desk, the metric that flags the *human* as the anomaly is visible early, so the Act-8 inversion is inevitable.
5. **Compressed the middle**: merged the two Mexico honeymoon functions and trimmed a redundant apartment beat.
6. **Last image is John** — the stutter now precedes the final frame but the film ends holding on John's face, not the glitch.
