# NOMAD — Ten Critiques on the DIALOGUE (of v1.2)

Ten craft perspectives, this time only on the spoken lines. Verdict + the single technique note. Consensus and integrations at the bottom; applied in v1.3.

---

**1. Sundance / indie naturalism.**
People don't speak in complete thoughts. Right now the lines are clean but a touch *written* — everyone finishes their sentences. Technique: **interrupt and overlap.** Let John step on Will, let Cairns trail off. The realest line is the half-said one.

**2. Cannes / auteur restraint.**
"It's the last one" is perfect because it withholds. But some lines still explain ("becoming the thing that pushed him out" is a stage direction leaking into speech territory). Technique: **cut any line that names the theme; trust the image.**

**3. A24 script editor (voice + quotability).**
Cairns is the voice engine but his sell is generic — "life on crack," "gotta get out here" could be any bro. Technique: **give each character one non-transferable verbal signature.** Cairns needs a specific tic (a word he overuses, a way he mangles a phrase) so a line is unmistakably his.

**4. Clermont-Ferrand (economy).**
The sell monologue is a few beats too long; it makes its point and keeps going. Technique: **land the pitch in three moves, not five.** Every extra "bro" dilutes the good ones.

**5. Nicholl reader (page voice).**
On the page, real-Cairns and likeness-Cairns read identically. The film's whole engine is that they're a half-degree apart — so the *writing* should encode the difference. Technique: **give the likeness a linguistic tell,** not just the 0.4 delay: too-complete grammar, mirrored phrasing, an idiom finished a hair too correctly. The reader should be able to spot the fake on paper.

**6. Fantastic Fest (the uncanny in speech).**
The machine's warmth should be *slightly wrong* in the mouth, not just late. Technique: **the likeness over-corrects.** Real Cairns drops words, slurs warmth ("Love you, buddy!"). The likeness restores them ("I love you, man.") — grammatically fuller, emotionally colder. That over-completion is the audible tell that pays off the handshake.

**7. Morelia (bilingual authenticity).**
The Mexican characters currently speak in "¡Sí!" and giggles — decoration, not voice. That's the reductive thing the film is supposed to critique, happening in its own dialogue. Technique: **give the local characters real lines in Spanish, subtitled, with edge and agency** — at least one that undercuts the American in the room. Camila especially must land a line, not a look.

**8. Playwright / dialogue doctor (rhythm).**
Good scenes have a rhythm of setup/turn. The John call has it ("You're in?" / "Course." / "It's gonna be everybody"). The sell doesn't — it's one voice pushing. Technique: **build the sell as call-and-response,** even against the girls or against Will's silence, so it has rhythm instead of volume.

**9. Actor / performance coach (playability).**
"Love you, bro" at the tell is doing everything; give the actor a physical anchor to play it against. Technique: **pin emotional lines to an action** (the handshake already does this — extend it: the likeness's warmest line lands while it's doing something a beat off).

**10. Contrarian / jaded.**
"You wouldn't believe it here" and "life on crack" are the lines a thousand nomad parodies already wrote. Technique: **kill the generic paradise-sell clichés; replace with specific, mundane, weirdly-true details** (the exact thing that would actually seduce a burnt-out fraud-ops guy — not margaritas, something smaller and realer).

---

## Consensus techniques to integrate

- **Interrupt / overlap / trail off** — break the clean sentences (1, 8).
- **Cut theme-naming lines** — trust image (2).
- **Give each character a non-transferable signature; kill generic sell** (3, 4, 10).
- **The likeness gets a LINGUISTIC tell** — over-completed grammar, mirrored phrasing (5, 6): real Cairns drops words, the fake restores them. Pays off with the handshake.
- **Real Spanish, real agency for the locals; Camila lands a line** (7).
- **Sell as call-and-response, not monologue** (8).

## Applied in v1.3

1. Cairns' sell tightened and made specific — one hyper-specific mundane detail replaces "life on crack"; his verbal signature established (so the likeness can later get it a hair wrong).
2. Real Cairns' warmth is sloppy and word-dropping throughout ("Love you, buddy!"). The LIKENESS at the midpoint over-corrects — "I love you, man." — grammatically complete, 0.4 late: an audible tell twinned with the handshake.
3. One of the two girls gets a real Spanish line (subtitled) that dryly undercuts Cairns — agency, not "¡Sí!".
4. Camila gets a specific subtitled line to her mother, not just "something furious."
5. Overlaps/interruptions marked in the John call and the sell.
6. Cut the theme-naming narration that had crept toward dialogue.
